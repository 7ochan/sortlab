# Phase 7 — Viva Preparation

Use these as concise, evidence-bound answers. Do not describe the simulated model as a physical prototype.

1. **Why choose SCARA?** The task is tabletop pick-and-place with mostly planar transfer and vertical insertion. Two rotary planar joints provide compact XY reach; a vertical prismatic axis handles height and a wrist yaw aligns jaws. A Cartesian gantry would be simpler and often stiffer, but needs a larger orthogonal frame and has less compact rotary reach. The choice is a conceptual fit, not a procurement comparison.
2. **How many DOF?** Four commanded axes: J1 and J2 revolute about parallel Z axes, J3 prismatic in world Z, J4 tool yaw about Z. It controls position and yaw for the modeled task, not arbitrary 6D orientation.
3. **What are the link lengths and reach?** L1=.30 m and L2=.25 m. Link-only ideal XY radii are |L1−L2|=.05 m to L1+L2=.55 m. Configured J2 limits and other joint/tool constraints shrink the feasible set; the measured grid is sampled, not a formal boundary.
4. **Give a FK example.** With q1=30°, q2=−60°, L1=.30 and L2=.25: x=.30cos30°+.25cos(−30°)=.476314 m; y=.30sin30°+.25sin(−30°)=.025000 m. With q3=.45 m and q4=15°, TCP is (.476314,.025000,.450000) m and yaw q1+q2+q4=−15°.
5. **How does IK work?** It computes `cos(q2)=(x²+y²−L1²−L2²)/(2L1L2)`, evaluates elbow branches, obtains q1 from target azimuth and link triangle, then derives J4 from desired yaw and checks joint limits. The 5,000 FK→IK→FK campaign had max position error 4.97e−16 m and angle error 1.78e−15 rad for sampled valid inputs; that is numerical software agreement, not physical accuracy.
6. **What happens at singularity?** At full extension the links are collinear and the Jacobian loses rank; the FK target is reachable but small Cartesian changes can require large joint changes. Full fold is not accepted under the configured ±160° J2 limit. The campaign tests these boundaries analytically.
7. **What actuators are selected?** None are purchased or sized. The design concept uses rotary electric servos for J1/J2/J4 and a linear electric drive for J3, with an electric parallel-jaw gripper. Motor torque, inertia, duty cycle, power supply and thermal sizing remain future work.
8. **How was grip force estimated?** A symmetric friction model gives `N=S m(g+a_z)/(2μ)`. For L: S=2, m=.60 kg, g=9.81 m/s², a_z=.30 m/s², μ=.50 gives 12.13 N per jaw; μ=.30 sensitivity gives 20.22 N/jaw. Capacity 25 N/jaw is only a virtual assumed limit. It proves neither actuator force nor safe real gripping.
9. **What code correction was made in Phase 7?** `attemptGrasp()` originally used gravity without the configured vertical acceleration, contrary to the baseline equation. It now uses g+a_z. A 12 N-cap regression confirms the L package is rejected at 12.13 N. The same nominal campaign still produced 100/100; the change did not loosen placement criteria.
10. **How is the trajectory generated?** The controller uses piecewise cubic joint-space profiles with endpoint boundary conditions, checks configured speed/acceleration, and samples the Cartesian route at fixed intervals. A cubic rest-to-rest scalar uses `q=q0+(qf−q0)(3u²−2u³)`. Joint space is easy to bound; Cartesian waypoints make task geometry intuitive but are converted/checked through IK and samples.
11. **What is the motion sequence?** Validate package/destination and route before motion; move to pickup; align and close; accept transport only after the grasp reports success and Rapier confirms an attachment; lift and travel through a clearance corridor; release; verify the actual detached package pose/velocity; return home; then complete.
12. **How does grasp succeed?** Both pad sensors must overlap, package center and yaw must fall within 12 mm and 8°, jaw width must fit, and the virtual safety-factored force check must pass. A Rapier fixed joint then tracks the package. This ideal joint has no slip, compliance, crush or actuator dynamics.
13. **What does placement verification require?** Actual package center must be inside the target bin’s interior region shrunk by package half extents and 5 mm; center Z must lie between floor/lip adjusted by package half-height and tolerances; linear speed must remain below .04 m/s and angular speed below .12 rad/s for .25 simulated seconds. Verification times out after 8 s. Correct bin, settled physics pose and completed controller state are all required.
14. **How does Rapier fit in?** It simulates package rigid-body motion and contact at fixed 1/60 s steps. Robot joints are kinematically commanded; there is no robot dynamics model. Repeated equal initial conditions and construction order produced identical 120-step traces in this environment, not a cross-platform guarantee.
15. **What are the main controller faults?** Unreachable route is rejected before motion; failed grasp has bounded retry; lost attachment, placement outside/wrong bin/timeout and invalid transitions become typed faults; user abort moves to a stopped terminal state. Reset detaches/resets the package/world and clears cycle state. Fault class evidence distinguishes controller hooks from actual physics behavior.
16. **What was the 100-cycle result?** 34 S→B1, 33 M→B2, 33 L→B3. All 100 reached completed with accepted target-bin placement, so 100/100 terminal attempts =100%, meeting the 95% nominal campaign target. This is a fixed-condition simulation campaign and not a 100-trial physical reliability estimate.
17. **What were cycle times?** Mean 30.680 s, median 30.350 s, range 29.917–31.783 simulated seconds. The baseline median goal of 12 s was missed. Setup and wall-clock execution time are excluded.
18. **What faults were tested?** Twelve classes, three trials each: route rejection, grasp miss, lost attachment, release response failure, wrong/outside placement, timeout, four abort phases and invalid transition. All 36 matched; no false placement; all reset clean and reran nominally. Hooks and explicit package teleport for location faults are injected tests, not natural failures.
19. **Are collisions proven safe?** No. TCP/trajectory and sampled constraints are tested; swept link/tool/package collision proof and reactive rigid-body robot collision handling are absent. This cannot be called collision-safe.
20. **What hazards matter?** Unexpected motion/pinch zones, dropped loads, collision with fixtures, and electrical/drive failure. A real cell needs guarding, interlocks, presence sensing, torque/force limits validated to standards, an independent safety-rated stop and formal risk assessment. The virtual stop is not an emergency stop.
21. **What is the biggest limitation?** No physical robot, camera, actuator, calibrated friction or robot dynamics. Package identity is deterministic/simulated; pouch is rigid; grip fixed joint is ideal; collision sweeps are incomplete; workcell assumes one package at a time.
22. **What would be improved next?** First achieve the 12 s cycle objective without compromising safe sampled constraints; add swept-volume collision analysis, realistic robot/drive dynamics, vision/ID uncertainty, slip/compliance and measured hardware parameters, then validate on hardware with safeguards. These are future work, not current features.
23. **Did a physical demonstration occur?** No physical build or hardware demonstration is in the evidence. The recorded browser smoke and campaigns verify the software simulation only. A live demo should be described accurately as a digital-twin simulation.
